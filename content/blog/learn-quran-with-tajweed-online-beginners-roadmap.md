If you want to learn Quran with Tajweed online but have no idea where to begin, you are in good company. Most beginners — children and adults alike — start with the same questions: do I need to know Arabic? What comes first? How long will it take before I can read on my own?

The good news is that the path is well travelled. It breaks down into clear stages, and with a patient teacher and a little daily practice, each one leads naturally to the next. Here is the roadmap we follow at Online Taleem ul Quran, from the very first letter to fluent recitation.

## Before you start: what a beginner really needs

You do **not** need to know any Arabic. Our beginner-friendly classes start with the basic Arabic letters and their pronunciation. What you do need is simple:

- **A live teacher** who can hear you and correct you. Tajweed is learned by ear, and mistakes are hard to notice on your own.
- **A device and a quiet corner** — our classes run over live video, and you can join from a computer, tablet or smartphone.
- **Ten to fifteen minutes a day** for revision between classes.
- **A starting point that fits you.** In a free demo class, a teacher listens to you read and suggests where to begin.

## Stage 1: The Qaida — letters, sounds and joining

Every reader of the Quran begins here. A *Qaida* is a step-by-step primer that takes you from single letters to joined letters, short and long vowels, *sukun*, *tanween* and *shaddah*, and finally to full words and verses.

At our academy, completing the Mastery Phase of our **Al-Burhan Qaida** is the foundation for every course that follows. It is tempting to rush this stage. Don’t: letters learned carefully now will save months of correction later.

## Stage 2: Tajweed foundations — correct pronunciation

Once you can read, you start learning to read *correctly*. Level 1 of our [Simplified Tajweed course](/course-details/simplified-tajweed) teaches you to pronounce each letter from its correct point of articulation (*makhraj*), along with basic attributes such as nasal sounds, *idgham*, *qalb*, the *madd* letters and the types of madd.

This is where your ear begins to change. Letters that once sounded the same — a light *sīn* and a heavy *ṣād*, a soft *hā’* and a breathy *ḥā’* — start to sound clearly different. If you want to understand why this matters so much, read our guide to [what Tajweed is and why it matters](/blog/what-is-tajweed-and-why-does-it-matter).

## Stage 3: Applying the Tajweed rules as you read

Knowing a rule is not the same as applying it. In Level 2, you practise the rules from Level 1 in real recitation, study the characteristics (*sifaat*) that tell similar letters apart, and learn to avoid common mistakes — such as stretching a sound where only a short vowel belongs.

Your teacher recites, you repeat, and your teacher corrects. It is the same method the Quran has always been taught with, and it works just as well over live video.

## Stage 4: Stopping, starting and tarteel

Level 3 brings everything together. You learn the rules of *waqf* — how and where to stop at the end or in the middle of a verse — revise everything from Levels 1 and 2, study some exceptions to the rules, and practise reciting with *tarteel*: slowly, clearly and deliberately.

> *Wa rattili l-Qur’āna tartīlā.*
>
> “And recite the Quran with measured recitation.”
>
> <cite>Quran 73:4</cite>

## Stage 5: Fluent Quran recitation

With Tajweed in place, the next goal is fluency: reading longer passages with clarity, confidence and beauty. Our [Quran Recitation course](/course-details/quran-recitation) focuses on the practical application of Tajweed rules, and also helps you learn the Arabic alphabet, grammar and vocabulary of the Quran — so that your connection with what you recite grows deeper, too.

## Common beginner mistakes to avoid

- **Rushing the Qaida.** Weak foundations show up later as stubborn pronunciation errors.
- **Learning only from videos.** Listening helps, but only a teacher can hear and correct *your* recitation.
- **Skipping daily practice.** Short, regular revision beats one long session before class.
- **Memorizing before the pronunciation is right.** A mistake learned by heart is much harder to remove.
- **Comparing yourself with others.** Everyone moves at their own pace — adults and children alike.

## Frequently asked questions for Tajweed beginners

### Can I learn Quran with Tajweed online without knowing Arabic?

Yes. No prior knowledge of Arabic is needed. Classes start with the basic Arabic letters and their pronunciation, and build up step by step.

### How long does it take to learn to read the Quran with Tajweed?

It depends on your starting point and how regularly you practise. Lessons move at your pace, and monthly exams help you and your teacher track progress and decide when you are ready for the next stage.

### What is the Al-Burhan Qaida?

It is the Qaida (reading primer) we use to teach the Arabic letters and the basics of reading. Completing its Mastery Phase is the prerequisite for our Tajweed, recitation and memorization courses.

### Is one-to-one or batch better for learning Tajweed?

Both are available. One-to-one classes give you the teacher’s full attention, which is especially helpful for pronunciation. Some students enjoy learning alongside others in a batch. Your teacher can advise you during the free demo class.
