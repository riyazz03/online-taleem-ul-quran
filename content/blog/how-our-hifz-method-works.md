Memorizing the Quran is one of the most beautiful goals a family can set. It is also a long journey, and the question parents ask us most often is a practical one: how does it actually work, class by class?

Our Quran memorization course follows an approach that has stood the test of time — preserved through generations and rooted in tradition. Every session is divided into three parts: **Sabaq**, **Sabaq Para** and **Purana Sabaq**. Understanding these three parts is the key to understanding how a student keeps memorizing more and more without forgetting what came before.

## Sabaq — the new lesson

*Sabaq* means the new lesson. The teacher first listens to the student recite the new verses and makes sure every word is pronounced correctly, with the right accent. Only then does memorization begin: the student repeats the verses many times until they are secure.

The teacher shares tips and techniques for memorizing, both during the class and for practising afterwards. The student’s task is simple but serious: **the new lesson must be fully memorized before the next class**.

Getting pronunciation right *before* memorizing matters. A mistake learned by heart is much harder to remove later than a mistake corrected on the first reading.

## Sabaq Para — revising the recent lessons

*Sabaq Para* is the revision of the lessons the student has memorized recently from the same juz. After the new lesson, the student recites these recent pages while the teacher listens and corrects.

Because it happens **in every class, straight after the new lesson**, Sabaq Para makes sure the newest verses do not slip away while they are still fresh — which is exactly when they are most at risk.

## Purana Sabaq — keeping old memorization alive

*Purana Sabaq* means “old lesson”: the revision of any juz the student has already memorized. In each class the student recites **at least half a juz** from their older memorization, and the teacher listens.

This is the part that gives both the student and the teacher real satisfaction and confidence. It shows that the student is memorizing more and more *without forgetting* the earlier parts — the true measure of a strong Hafiz.

## Why the three parts belong together

Each part has a different job:

- **Sabaq** moves the student forward.
- **Sabaq Para** fixes the newest verses firmly in place.
- **Purana Sabaq** protects everything memorized before.

A student who only races ahead with new lessons may reach the end of a juz quickly, only to find that its beginning has faded. A student who only revises never moves forward. Balancing all three in every class is what makes steady, lasting progress possible.

## Practical tips for parents

Your support at home makes an enormous difference. These simple habits help young memorizers thrive:

- **Keep a fixed daily time.** Many families find the early morning, after Fajr, is when the mind is freshest.
- **Use the same Mushaf every day.** Seeing each verse in the same place on the same page helps visual memory.
- **Listen to the sabaq before class.** Five minutes of listening to your child recite shows you both where the weak spots are.
- **Protect revision time.** When time is short, it is tempting to skip Sabaq Para or Purana Sabaq. Don’t — revision is what keeps the memorization.
- **Keep sessions short and focused.** Several short practice sessions are better than one long, tiring one.
- **Encourage, never compare.** Every child memorizes at their own pace. Praise effort and consistency, not just speed.
- **Celebrate milestones.** Finishing a surah or a juz deserves a small celebration and a heartfelt du‘a together.
- **Stay in touch with the teacher.** Ask how the revision is going, not only how many new pages were covered.

## A reward that rises with every verse

The Prophet ﷺ described the honour that awaits the companion of the Quran:

> يُقَالُ لِصَاحِبِ الْقُرْآنِ اقْرَأْ وَارْتَقِ وَرَتِّلْ كَمَا كُنْتَ تُرَتِّلُ فِي الدُّنْيَا فَإِنَّ مَنْزِلَكَ عِنْدَ آخِرِ آيَةٍ تَقْرَؤُهَا
>
> “The Messenger of Allah (ﷺ) said: One who was devoted to the Qur'an will be told to recite, ascend and recite carefully as he recited carefully when he was in the world, for he will reach his abode when he comes to the last verse he recites.”
>
> <cite>Sunan Abi Dawud 1464</cite>

And memorizing even a few verses carries its own protection:

> “Whoever memorizes ten verses from the beginning of Surah Al Kahf, he will be protected from the Dajjal.”
>
> <cite>Sahih Muslim</cite>
